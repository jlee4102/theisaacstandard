## What it is

The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC are true wireless earbuds from Anker's audio sub-brand, positioned at the $100 price point. They aim to deliver premium active noise cancellation (ANC) and high-resolution audio support at a fraction of the cost of flagship competitors. The "NC" in the name stands for noise cancellation, which is the headline feature here.

These earbuds sit above the more basic Soundcore Life series but below the flagship Liberty 4 (which adds spatial audio and a higher price tag). They compete directly with offerings from Samsung, Nothing, and EarFun in the increasingly crowded mid-range market. The Liberty 4 NC are available in several colors and ship with a wireless charging case.

## What works

The ANC performance is the standout feature. Independent testing confirms the Liberty 4 NC delivers noise cancellation that rivals earbuds costing twice as much. The adaptive system adjusts in real-time based on your environment—it's not perfect, but it handles consistent low-frequency noise like airplane engines, bus rumble, and office HVAC systems exceptionally well. For $100, this level of isolation is genuinely impressive.

Sound quality is solid, with a consumer-friendly tuning that emphasizes bass without overwhelming the mids. The 11mm drivers produce a warm, energetic sound that works well for pop, hip-hop, and electronic music. The real differentiator is LDAC support, which is rare at this price point. If you have an Android device that supports LDAC, you get near-lossless audio streaming—a tangible upgrade over standard AAC or SBC codecs. Audiophiles will appreciate the detail retrieval, though the soundstage is average for the category.

Battery life is generous. With ANC off, the earbuds last up to 10 hours per charge, and the case adds another 40 hours. Even with ANC on, you get 8 hours, which is enough for a full workday or a long flight. The case supports Qi wireless charging, a convenience feature usually reserved for more expensive models.

The Soundcore app is well-designed and functional. You get a customizable EQ with presets, HearID (which tailors the sound profile to your hearing), and control customization. The app also lets you switch between ANC modes, transparency mode, and normal mode.

## What doesn't

The touch controls are overly sensitive. I frequently triggered play/pause or volume changes just by adjusting the earbuds in my ears. You can disable individual gestures in the app, but the default sensitivity is too high for comfortable everyday use. This is a common complaint across user reviews, and it's not something you can fully fix with settings.

Fit is inconsistent. The earbuds are relatively small at 4.9g each, but the shape doesn't suit all ears. Users with smaller ear canals may find them loose or uncomfortable after 1-2 hours. The included ear tips help, but I recommend trying third-party foam tips if you experience discomfort. The lack of ear fins or wings means stability relies entirely on the tip seal.

Call quality is average. The six-microphone array does a decent job in quiet environments, but background noise reduction is less effective than competitors like the Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro. In windy conditions or busy streets, callers reported that my voice sounded distant and muffled. If you take frequent calls in noisy places, look elsewhere.

## How it compares

**Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro** ($189.99): More refined ANC and significantly better call quality. The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro also offer a more comfortable fit for most ears and better integration with Samsung devices. But they cost nearly double. The Liberty 4 NC delivers comparable ANC performance for less than half the price, making them the better value for budget-focused buyers.

**EarFun Air Pro 3** ($79.99): Cheaper, with similar ANC and battery life. However, the EarFun's sound quality is noticeably thinner, with less bass extension and detail. Build quality also feels cheaper. The Liberty 4 NC is a clear step up in audio and ANC, justifying the $20 premium.

**Nothing Ear (2)** ($149.99): More stylish design with a transparent aesthetic and a better app experience. The Nothing Ear (2) also has a more neutral sound signature that some will prefer. But ANC is weaker, battery life is shorter (6 hours with ANC), and the price is 50% higher. The Liberty 4 NC wins on noise cancellation and overall value.

## Who should buy this

If you're an Android user on a budget who wants strong ANC for commuting, travel, or noisy open offices, the Liberty 4 NC is an easy recommendation. The LDAC support is a genuine bonus for high-resolution audio enthusiasts. You're getting ANC that punches well above its price class, good battery life, and wireless charging—all for $100.

## Final verdict

The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC is a rare example of a budget product that doesn't feel like a compromise. The ANC is genuinely excellent, the sound quality is good (especially with LDAC), and the battery life is generous. The touch controls and fit issues are real annoyances, but they don't undermine the core value proposition.

For $100, you're getting noise cancellation that rivals $200 earbuds. That's a win. If you can live with the occasional accidental touch and need to experiment with ear tips, these are among the best budget ANC earbuds available today.
